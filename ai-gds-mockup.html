Artificial Intelligence

We teach students to think with AI, about AI, and beyond AI. The goal is never a faster answer; it is a deeper thinker.

Georgetown Day School was founded in 1945 by families who refused to accept the world as it was. That founding spirit shapes how we approach artificial intelligence: not with hype, and not with fear, but with the same clear-eyed conviction that powerful forces must be examined, understood, and bent toward human dignity.

Our philosophy is simple to state and demanding to practice. AI at GDS must serve the capacities we exist to develop in students: to think constructively, to lead ethically, and to collaborate courageously. Where a tool deepens curiosity, sharpens reasoning, or expands what a student can attempt, we embrace it. Where it substitutes for the productive struggle that learning requires, we set it aside. The human being, in every case, stays at the center.

This is not a policy written once and shelved. Faculty, students, and families are engaged in an ongoing, structured exploration of what AI means for teaching, learning, and integrity, guided by published principles, informed by current research, and revised as the technology and the evidence evolve.

Our Working Community

The AI Explorers

A cross-divisional community of GDS teachers, staff, and students charged with exploring AI thoughtfully and advising school leadership on where we go next.

Exploration as a Discipline

The AI Explorers are GDS's standing answer to a fast-moving question. Rather than outsourcing our AI direction to vendors or headlines, we convene the people who know our classrooms best: faculty and staff from every division, joined by student voices and connected to parent conversations throughout the year. The group meets monthly, shares how AI is actually being used across the school, pressure-tests our draft principles against real classroom cases, and delivers recommendations directly to the senior leadership team.

Between full gatherings, the Explorers work in four focused groups, each with a plain-language charge:

Think Constructively

Pedagogical Integrity

"Keep the human in learning."

Defines when AI is a power tool and when it is a crutch, and builds the rubric teachers use to decide whether a tool helps students think or thinks for them.

Lead Ethically

Ethics & Intellectual Property

"Lead with integrity."

Writes the playbook for the digital frontier: citation and academic-integrity guidelines, student data privacy, and honest treatment of intellectual property.

Collaborate Courageously

Professional Growth & Literacy

"Learn out loud."

Maps the AI literacy journey for every adult in the building and designs the showcases where colleagues share their wins and their instructive failures.

Augmenting Capacity

Efficiency

"Buy back time."

Vets tools that lift administrative weight off teachers, building a living productivity toolkit so more faculty time flows to students.

The Explorers' work feeds a public rhythm: guiding principles published to this site, community conversations with families each spring, and an annual set of recommendations that shapes the school's platform and policy decisions for the year ahead.

Three Commitments Guide Every Decision

Every tool we adopt, every classroom norm we set, and every policy we publish is tested against the three capacities at the heart of a GDS education.

Think Constructively

AI must be a bicycle for the mind, not a chauffeur. Students learn when to use these tools as a thought partner and when to set them down, because the reasoning, drafting, and struggling is the point.

Lead Ethically

Students interrogate how AI systems are built, whose data trains them, where bias hides, and what honest attribution looks like. Integrity is taught explicitly, not assumed.

Collaborate Courageously

Teachers, students, and families learn out loud together, sharing wins and honest failures. No one at GDS navigates this technology alone, and no one gets left behind.

An Evidence-Based Approach

What the Research Says, and What We Do About It

Schools everywhere are making AI decisions on instinct. We prefer evidence. Our practice is grounded in the leading research on AI and learning, and it changes when the evidence does.

Unstructured AI use can undermine learning

Stanford's 2026 review of the K-12 evidence base found that students using general-purpose chatbots often perform well during practice but worse on independent assessment; pedagogical guardrails eliminated that gap.

At GDS: teachers, not tools, decide when and how AI enters an assignment, using a faculty-built rubric that asks one question first: does this help the student think, or think for the student?

Source: Stanford SCALE Initiative, The Evidence Base on AI in K-12 (2026)

Productive struggle is where durable learning lives

Research on cognitive offloading shows that when AI removes too much effort, reasoning quality and retention decline, even as students report enjoying the work more. Ease is not the same as learning.

At GDS: we deliberately protect the hard parts, including first drafts, problem sets, and open questions, and position AI where it extends a student's reach rather than replacing the climb.

Sources: peer-reviewed studies on generative AI and critical thinking (2025-26)

AI literacy is a defined, teachable competency

UNESCO's global frameworks define what students and teachers actually need: a human-centered mindset, ethical judgment, technical understanding, and creative application, woven across disciplines rather than siloed in computer science.

At GDS: AI literacy spans the curriculum, from age-appropriate foundations in Lower School to Upper Level coursework like Advanced Coding for Artificial Intelligence in the High School.

Source: UNESCO AI Competency Frameworks for Students and Teachers (2024)

AI can give teachers time back for students

Controlled studies show teachers using AI with training cut lesson-preparation time by roughly a third with no measurable loss in lesson quality, and that coaching benefits accrue most to those still building their craft.

At GDS: our faculty working groups vet and share time-saving tools so that every hour AI returns goes where it belongs: to relationships with students.

Source: Stanford SCALE Initiative, The Evidence Base on AI in K-12 (2026)

From the Classroom to the Business Office

The Tools We Provide, and How We Use Them Ourselves

A philosophy is only as good as its implementation. Here is what we put in teachers' hands, and how the school holds its own operations to the standard we set for students: use AI where it multiplies human effort, never where it replaces human judgment.

In the Classroom: One Platform, Chosen Deliberately

Every GDS teacher receives a school-provided subscription to Google Gemini and NotebookLM.

We chose a primary platform on purpose. Gemini and NotebookLM operate under the school's enterprise controls, which means faculty and student data is governed by our agreements rather than by a consumer product's fine print. NotebookLM grounds its responses in the sources a teacher provides, which makes it unusually well suited to real learning. And because both are integrated across the Google Workspace tools GDS already runs on, AI assistance lives where the work already happens.

Where a role calls for something more specialized, additional AI tools are provisioned for faculty and staff as their work requires, each vetted through the same privacy and pedagogy review.

Behind the Scenes: Efficiency in Service of the Program

We hold our operations to the same standard we teach.

Beyond the classroom, GDS actively pursues AI to make the school itself run better: automating repetitive administrative tasks, streamlining workflows across departments, and using AI-assisted development to build custom tools and platforms where commercial solutions do not exist or do not fit our needs.

The point is not technology for its own sake. Every hour and dollar these efficiencies recover is redirected toward what families send their children here for in the first place: teaching, learning, and the program itself.

Pre-K Through 12th Grade

A Developmental Approach, By Design

What is appropriate for a senior building a machine-learning model is not appropriate for a first grader, and our approach honors that. AI literacy at GDS unfolds the way everything here does: developmentally, deliberately, and in step with who students are becoming.

Lower School

Foundations without screens in the way. Our youngest students build the bedrock of AI literacy: asking good questions, distinguishing people from machines, and understanding that computers follow human instructions.

Students do not use generative AI tools independently. Teachers may use AI behind the scenes to enrich planning, always with the same care they bring to everything in the classroom.

Middle School

Guided exploration and digital judgment. As students' capacity for abstraction grows, so does the conversation: how recommendation systems shape what they see, what a model does with their data, and why a confident answer is not always a correct one.

Any classroom AI use is structured, supervised, and connected to the skills being taught, never a substitute for them.

High School

Fluency, discernment, and creation. High schoolers engage AI where teachers have deliberately designed for it, with clear norms for attribution and integrity, and study it directly in courses like Advanced Coding for Artificial Intelligence.

Student focus groups inform our policies, because students who will graduate into an AI-shaped world deserve a voice in how their school meets it.

Questions Families Ask

Does GDS allow students to use AI on schoolwork?+
It depends on the assignment, and that is by design. Teachers set explicit expectations for each course and task, and students are taught to treat undisclosed AI use the way we treat any misrepresentation of their work. Where AI is invited in, the purpose is always to deepen the work, not to shortcut it.
How does GDS protect student privacy with AI tools?+
Every tool we consider is reviewed for how it handles student data before it comes near a classroom, consistent with our obligations to families and with the standards our Ethics & Intellectual Property working group maintains. Being cautious with children's data is not a compliance exercise for us; it is a values statement.
Is GDS worried AI will erode critical thinking?+
We take that risk seriously because the research says we should. That is precisely why our approach centers on protecting productive struggle and teaching students when not to use these tools. The families who founded GDS did not respond to hard questions by looking away, and neither do we.
How can parents be part of this conversation?+
Families are one of the four communities we convene. We host parent gatherings on AI at GDS during the school year, publish our guiding principles and resources on this site, and welcome questions anytime. The short version: you are not audience to this work; you are part of it.

Serious About the Future. Joyful About Learning.

GDS has spent eighty years teaching students to meet the world as it is and to make it better than they found it. Artificial intelligence is simply the newest place we keep that promise.
